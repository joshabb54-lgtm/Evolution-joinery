City centre kitchens — what makes them different

City centre kitchen fits often come with challenges you do not get on a modern housing estate. Access can be tight — narrow entries, steep staircases, and restricted parking. Period features like chimney breasts, alcoves, and sloped ceilings need to be worked around. Party walls mean you need to be considerate about noise and dust. Listed building status can restrict what can be fixed and how.

We are used to all of this. When we survey a city centre property, we think carefully about access logistics, how we will manage dust and mess in a terraced property, and how to make the most of kitchens that are often compact by modern standards. Bespoke cabinetry is frequently the answer for city centre kitchens where standard units simply will not fill the space well.

Frequently asked questions

Can you fit kitchens in listed buildings in York?
Yes, with care. Listed building consent governs structural changes, but kitchen fitting that does not affect the fabric of the building is generally permissible. We advise on a case-by-case basis and can work within listed building constraints.
How do you handle access for city centre properties?
We plan access routes before starting. For terraced properties with narrow entries, we carry units through in parts if needed. For apartments with stairs only, we plan deliveries and carrying routes carefully to avoid damage.
Do you work in apartments as well as houses?
Yes. Apartment kitchens in York city centre are a regular part of our work — they often have open-plan layouts where the kitchen needs to look considered from the living space as well as function well.
What areas of the city centre do you cover?
All of York city centre — Micklegate, Bishophill, Bootham, Gillygate, Goodramgate, Fossgate, Skeldergate, and surrounding streets. If you are not sure whether your address falls within our area, just call us.
