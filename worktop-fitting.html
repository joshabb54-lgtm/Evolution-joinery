A worktop is one of the most used surfaces in your home. It takes daily punishment from pots, pans, knives, water, and heat — and it needs to be fitted well to hold up over time. A poorly cut joint lifts at the seam. A worktop not properly supported sags. A cut not made cleanly at the hob or sink looks amateurish and lets water in.

At Evolution Joinery, we fit worktops of all materials across York and the surrounding area. Whether you are fitting a new kitchen or replacing an existing worktop, we measure carefully, cut accurately, and finish cleanly every time.

Worktop materials we fit

Different worktop materials have different requirements for cutting, jointing, and fitting. We work with all of the following:

  • Laminate worktops — The most common choice for fitted kitchens. We cut and join laminate accurately, use the correct jointing strips and sealants, and make clean cutouts for hobs and sinks.
  • Solid wood worktops — Oak, walnut, and beech are the most popular. Solid wood expands and contracts with humidity and needs to be fitted with this in mind. We use the correct fixings and oiling process.
  • Quartz worktops — Quartz is cut and polished by the supplier, but we handle the final fitting, templating, and positioning. We work with quartz suppliers across Yorkshire.
  • Granite and stone worktops — Same process as quartz. Heavy, precise, and unforgiving of errors — we take the time to get it right.
  • Dekton and porcelain worktops — Ultra-compact surfaces that require care when cutting and positioning. We have experience with these materials.

What worktop fitting involves

A worktop fit is more involved than it looks. Here is the process we follow on every job:

  • Templating — We measure the run accurately, accounting for walls that are not perfectly straight and corners that may not be exactly 90 degrees.
  • Cutting — Precise cuts for length, hob cutouts, and sink cutouts. Rough cuts leave edges that chip and are hard to seal.
  • Jointing — Where two runs of worktop meet, the joint needs to be tight, level, and properly sealed. A good joint is nearly invisible.
  • Fitting and securing — Worktops are secured to base units with the correct fixings for the material. Solid wood requires slotted brackets to allow movement.
  • Finishing — Edges are finished to the wall with silicone. Sink apertures and hob cutouts are sealed. Solid wood worktops are oiled.
Signs of a quality worktop fit
  • Joints are flush and tight with no visible gap
  • Hob and sink cutouts are clean with no chipping
  • Worktop is level across its full length
  • Silicone seal to wall is neat and even
  • No movement or flex when pressing down on the surface
  • Overhang is consistent along the full run

Frequently asked questions

Can you fit a worktop I have already purchased?
Yes. If you have already sourced your worktop, we can fit it. Just make sure to let us know the material and supplier so we can bring the right tools and fixings.
Do you fit quartz and granite worktops?
We handle the fitting and positioning of quartz and stone worktops. These are typically cut and polished by the supplier — we work alongside your supplier or can recommend fabricators we have worked with in Yorkshire.
How long does worktop fitting take?
A standard kitchen worktop run typically takes one day. More complex layouts with multiple runs, an island, or unusual angles may take longer.
Can you replace just the worktop without changing the cabinets?
Yes — worktop replacement is a common job. We remove the old worktop, make good any damage to the unit tops, and fit the new worktop cleanly.
Do you fit upstands and splashbacks?
We fit worktop upstands (matching or contrasting strips that run up the wall behind the worktop). Full-height splashbacks in glass, tile, or matching material can also be arranged.
