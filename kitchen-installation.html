What a full kitchen installation involves

A complete kitchen installation is not just about putting cabinets on walls. It involves careful planning, precise measurement, and a methodical approach to ensure everything lines up, sits level, and functions as it should. Here is what we cover as standard:

  • Unit assembly — Base units, wall units, and tall cabinets assembled and positioned correctly before anything is fixed.
  • Cabinet installation — Units fixed securely to walls and floors, levelled carefully regardless of what the floor or wall is doing.
  • Door and drawer fitting — Hinges and runners adjusted so every door hangs true and every drawer opens and closes without catching.
  • Plinth fitting — Base plinths cut and fitted neatly to the floor line, with corner pieces and end caps where needed.
  • Cornice and pelmet — Top-of-cabinet finishing strips fitted and mitred for a clean, professional look.
  • Infill panels and end panels — Exposed cabinet sides finished with matching panels, cut accurately to size.
  • Worktop fitting — See our dedicated worktop fitting page for full detail on materials and process.
  • Appliance integration — Integrated appliances built in flush and secured correctly. More detail on our appliance integration page.

Why the quality of the fit matters

Most people focus on choosing the right style, colour, and units — and rightly so. But the difference between a kitchen that looks good in photos and one that actually works beautifully every day comes down to how it is fitted.

Poorly fitted kitchens show their age fast. Doors drift out of alignment. Gaps appear at the wall. Worktops lift at joints. Plinths do not sit flush because the floor was not accounted for properly. None of these are inevitable — they are consequences of rushing or cutting corners on the installation.

We take the time to do things properly. That means checking levels constantly, scribing panels to walls that are not perfectly straight, and fitting each component in the right order so nothing has to be undone later. It is not glamorous work, but it is the difference between a kitchen that looks right and one that actually is right.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a full kitchen installation take?
Most kitchen installations take between two and five days depending on the size of the kitchen, the number of units, and whether appliances need integrating. We will give you a realistic timeframe when we quote.
Do I need to have my kitchen delivered before you start?
Yes — we need all units, doors, and hardware on site before we begin. We can advise on delivery timing and what to check when your kitchen arrives to spot any damaged or missing parts before the fitting date.
Do you work with IKEA and flat-pack kitchens?
Absolutely. We fit kitchens from all major suppliers including IKEA, Howdens, Wren, Magnet, and B&Q. The assembly and installation process is the same regardless of supplier.
Can you remove the old kitchen as well?
Yes. Full kitchen strip-out and disposal can be arranged as part of the job. See our kitchen renovation page for more detail.
Do you fit worktops and appliances as well?
Yes — worktop fitting and appliance integration are part of our service. We have dedicated pages on worktop fitting and appliance integration if you want more detail.
